Much of the lab's research aims to understand the biology that underlies dispositional anxiety. One line of this work studies behavioral inhibition in nonhuman primates, using multi-modal neuroimaging together with machine-learning-based behavioral analysis. The NIH-funded project behind it (R01 MH121735, PI: Fox) is titled "Origins and Emergence of Maladaptive Socioemotional Behavior During the Transition to Adulthood in Primates."

A related pilot study, supported by the UC Davis Behavioral Health Center of Excellence, aims to provide a proof of principle for wireless, projection-specific optogenetic manipulation in nonhuman primates. It is described as the first study to manipulate cortical projections to subcortical structures involved in the generation of anxiety.
